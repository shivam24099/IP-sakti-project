# Patentability Basics in India

For an invention to be patentable in India under the Patents Act, 1970, it generally must be novel, involve an inventive step, and be capable of industrial application.

Traditional knowledge and things that are already publicly known -- such as a formulation described in classical Ayurvedic texts -- cannot usually be patented on their own, since they lack novelty. However, a genuinely new and inventive improvement, extraction method, or specific application derived from traditional knowledge may be patentable if it meets the standard novelty and inventive-step requirements.

Applicants should also be aware that Section 3 of the Patents Act lists certain subject matter that is not considered an invention, including the mere discovery of a new use for a known substance.
